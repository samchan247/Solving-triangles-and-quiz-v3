Solving Triangles & Calculating Area (Complete Interactive Presentation)

Slide 1: Fundamental Formulas Reference

1. The Law of Sines

\[\frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B} = \frac{c}{\sin C}\]

Used primarily for ASA, AAS, and SSA (ambiguous) cases.

2. The Law of Cosines

\[a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc \cos A, \quad \cos A = \frac{b^2 + c^2 - a^2}{2bc}\] \[b^2 = a^2 + c^2 - 2ac \cos B, \quad \cos B = \frac{a^2 + c^2 - b^2}{2ac}\] \[c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab \cos C, \quad \cos C = \frac{a^2 + b^2 - c^2}{2ab}\]

Used primarily for SSS and SAS cases.

3. Area Formulas

Standard Formula: \(\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}\)

SAS Formula: \(\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}ab \sin C = \frac{1}{2}bc \sin A = \frac{1}{2}ac \sin B\)

ASA / AAS Formula: \(\text{Area} = \frac{c^2 \sin A \sin B}{2 \sin C}\)

Heron's Formula (SSS): \(\text{Area} = \sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}\) where \(s = \frac{a+b+c}{2}\)

Golden Rule: Standard notation assumes triangle \(\triangle ABC\) with side lengths \(a, b, c\) opposite to angles \(\angle A, \angle B, \angle C\). Always ensure angle arguments are evaluated in degrees or radians matching your computational setup.

Slide 2: SSS – Acute Triangle

Example 1: Given \(a = 5\), \(b = 6\), \(c = 7\) in \(\triangle ABC\)

Step 1: Find angle \(\angle A\) using Law of Cosines

\[\cos A = \frac{b^2 + c^2 - a^2}{2bc} = \frac{6^2 + 7^2 - 5^2}{2(6)(7)} = \frac{60}{84} \implies \angle A \approx 44.42^\circ\]

Step 2: Find angle \(\angle B\) using Law of Cosines

\[\cos B = \frac{a^2 + c^2 - b^2}{2ac} = \frac{5^2 + 7^2 - 6^2}{2(5)(7)} = \frac{38}{70} \implies \angle B \approx 57.12^\circ\]

Step 3: Find angle \(\angle C\) using Angle Sum

\[\angle C = 180^\circ - 44.42^\circ - 57.12^\circ = 78.46^\circ\]

Step 4: Area using Heron's Formula

\[s = \frac{5+6+7}{2} = 9 \implies \text{Area} = \sqrt{9(4)(3)(2)} = \sqrt{216} \approx 14.70\]

Slide 5: SAS – Acute Angle Included

Example 4: Given \(b = 6\), \(c = 8\), \(\angle A = 40^\circ\) in \(\triangle ABC\)

\[a = \sqrt{6^2 + 8^2 - 2(6)(8)\cos(40^\circ)} = \sqrt{100 - 96(0.7660)} = \sqrt{26.46} \approx 5.14\]

Step 2: Find angle \(\angle B\) using Law of Sines

\[\sin B = \frac{b \sin A}{a} = \frac{6 \sin(40^\circ)}{5.14} \approx \frac{3.8567}{5.14} \approx 0.7503 \implies \angle B \approx 48.62^\circ\]

Step 3: Find angle \(\angle C\) using Angle Sum

\[\angle C = 180^\circ - 40^\circ - 48.62^\circ = 91.38^\circ\]

Step 4: Area Calculation

\[\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}bc \sin A = \frac{1}{2}(6)(8)\sin(40^\circ) \approx 15.43\]

Slide 6: SAS – Obtuse Angle Included

Example 5: Given \(p = 4\), \(q = 6\), \(\angle R = 110^\circ\) in \(\triangle PQR\)

Step 1: Find side \(r\) using Law of Cosines

\[r = \sqrt{4^2 + 6^2 - 2(4)(6)\cos(110^\circ)} = \sqrt{16 + 36 - 48(-0.3420)} = \sqrt{68.42} \approx 8.27\]

Step 2: Find angle \(\angle P\) using Law of Sines

\[\sin P = \frac{p \sin R}{r} = \frac{4 \sin(110^\circ)}{8.27} \approx \frac{3.7588}{8.27} \approx 0.4545 \implies \angle P \approx 27.03^\circ\]

Step 3: Find angle \(\angle Q\) using Angle Sum

\[\angle Q = 180^\circ - 110^\circ - 27.03^\circ = 42.97^\circ\]

Step 4: Area Calculation

\[\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}pq \sin R = \frac{1}{2}(4)(6)\sin(110^\circ) \approx 11.28\]

\[\angle C = 180^\circ - 50^\circ - 60^\circ = 70^\circ\]

Step 2: Find side \(a\) using Law of Sines

\[a = \frac{c \sin A}{\sin C} = \frac{10 \sin(50^\circ)}{\sin(70^\circ)} \approx \frac{7.6604}{0.9397} \approx 8.15\]

\[b = \frac{c \sin B}{\sin C} = \frac{10 \sin(60^\circ)}{\sin(70^\circ)} \approx \frac{8.6603}{0.9397} \approx 9.22\]

Step 4: Area Calculation

\[\text{Area} = \frac{c^2 \sin A \sin B}{2 \sin C} = \frac{10^2 \sin(50^\circ) \sin(60^\circ)}{2 \sin(70^\circ)} \approx 35.28\]

Slide 10: AAS – Acute Angle Case

Step 4: Area Calculation

\[\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}ab \sin C = \frac{1}{2}(8)(11.70)\sin(70^\circ) \approx 44.00\]

Slide 12: SSA – Ambiguous Case (Two Solutions)

Example 11: Given \(\angle A = 30^\circ\), \(a = 7\), \(b = 10\) in \(\triangle ABC\)

Step 1: Check height condition \(h = b \sin A = 10 \sin(30^\circ) = 5\)

Since \(h < a < b\) (\(5 < 7 < 10\)), exactly TWO valid triangles exist!

Solution 1 (Acute \(\angle B_1\)):

\[\sin B_1 = \frac{b \sin A}{a} = \frac{10(0.5)}{7} = 0.7143 \implies \angle B_1 \approx 45.58^\circ\] \[\angle C_1 = 180^\circ - 30^\circ - 45.58^\circ = 104.42^\circ\]

Solution 2 (Obtuse \(\angle B_2\)):

\[\angle B_2 = 180^\circ - 45.58^\circ = 134.42^\circ\] \[\angle C_2 = 180^\circ - 30^\circ - 134.42^\circ = 15.58^\circ\]

Practice MC Questions

Q1. Given \(\angle A = 30^\circ\), \(b = 12\). What range of side \(a\) gives 2 solutions?
Hint: Height \(h = 12 \sin(30^\circ) = 6\). Two solutions exist when \(h < a < b\).
Golden Rule: SSA yields two solutions when \(b \sin A < a < b\).

Slide 13: SSA – Unique Solution Case

Example 12: Given \(\angle A = 30^\circ\), \(a = 12\), \(b = 8\) in \(\triangle ABC\)

Step 1: Compare side lengths

Since \(a \ge b\) (\(12 \ge 8\)), side \(a\) can only swing across the base in one direction, yielding EXACTLY ONE valid triangle.

Step 2: Solve unique triangle

\[\sin B = \frac{b \sin A}{a} = \frac{8 \sin(30^\circ)}{12} = \frac{4}{12} = 0.3333 \implies \angle B \approx 19.47^\circ\] \[\angle C = 180^\circ - 30^\circ - 19.47^\circ = 130.53^\circ\] \[c = \frac{a \sin C}{\sin A} = \frac{12 \sin(130.53^\circ)}{\sin(30^\circ)} \approx 18.24\]

Practice MC Questions

Q1. If \(\angle A = 40^\circ\), \(b = 10\), and \(a = 15\), how many triangles are formed?
Hint: When \(a \ge b\), there is always exactly one unique solution.
Golden Rule: If \(a \ge b\), the SSA ambiguity vanishes and produces 1 solution.

Slide 14: SSA – No Solution Case

Example 13: Given \(\angle A = 30^\circ\), \(a = 4\), \(b = 10\) in \(\triangle ABC\)

Step 1: Calculate vertical altitude \(h\)

\[h = b \sin A = 10 \sin(30^\circ) = 5\]

Step 2: Compare side length \(a\) to altitude \(h\)

Since \(a < h\) (\(4 < 5\)), side \(a\) is too short to reach the base line. NO TRIANGLE CAN BE FORMED!

\[\sin B = \frac{10 \sin(30^\circ)}{4} = \frac{5}{4} = 1.25 > 1 \quad (\text{Impossible!})\]

Practice MC Questions

Q1. Given \(\angle A = 45^\circ\), \(b = 10\). Which value of \(a\) forms NO triangle?
Hint: Minimum height \(h = 10 \sin(45^\circ) \approx 7.07\). Any \(a < 7.07\) gives no solution.
Golden Rule: If \(a < b \sin A\), \(\sin B > 1\), meaning no triangle exists.

Slide 15: Practical Application – Bearings & Distances

Example 14: Navigation Problem in \(\triangle PQR\)

A ship departs Point P on bearing 060° for 10 km to Q, then turns on bearing 150° for 15 km to R.

Step 1: Find included interior angle \(\angle Q\)

\[\angle Q = (180^\circ - 60^\circ) + 150^\circ = 120^\circ + 150^\circ = 270^\circ \implies \text{Interior } \angle Q = 360^\circ - 270^\circ = 90^\circ\]

Step 2: Calculate direct distance PR

\[PR = \sqrt{10^2 + 15^2} = \sqrt{100 + 225} = \sqrt{325} \approx 18.03\text{ km}\]

Practice MC Questions

Hint: Apply Pythagorean theorem since interior angle at Q is \(90^\circ\).
Golden Rule: Draw parallel North lines at each vertex to find interior angles.

Slide 16: Composite Figure & Quadrilateral Area

Example 15: Area of Quadrilateral ABCD

Split quadrilateral ABCD into \(\triangle ABC\) and \(\triangle ADC\) using diagonal AC.

Step 1: Area of \(\triangle ABC\) (Given \(AB=6, BC=8, \angle B=60^\circ\))

\[\text{Area}_1 = \frac{1}{2}(6)(8)\sin(60^\circ) = 24(0.8660) \approx 20.78\]

Step 2: Find diagonal length AC using Law of Cosines

\[AC = \sqrt{6^2 + 8^2 - 2(6)(8)\cos(60^\circ)} = \sqrt{100 - 48} = \sqrt{52} \approx 7.21\]

Step 3: Area of \(\triangle ADC\) using Heron's Formula (\(AD=9, CD=7, AC=7.21\))

\[s = \frac{9 + 7 + 7.21}{2} = 11.605 \implies \text{Area}_2 \approx 24.87\]

Total Quadrilateral Area: \(20.78 + 24.87 = 45.65\)

Hint: Apply Law of Cosines on \(\triangle ABC\): \(AC = \sqrt{6^2+8^2-2(6)(8)\cos 60^\circ}\).
Golden Rule: Divide complex 2D polygons into non-overlapping triangles.

Slide 17: Master Summary & Decision Tree

Triangle Solving Strategy Cheat Sheet

  • SSS: Law of Cosines for largest angle first \(\to\) Law of Cosines/Sines \(\to\) Heron's Formula.
  • SAS: Law of Cosines for missing side \(\to\) Law of Sines for smaller angle \(\to\) Area = \(\frac{1}{2}ab \sin C\).
  • ASA / AAS: Angle Sum Theorem (\(180^\circ - A - B\)) \(\to\) Law of Sines for sides \(\to\) ASA Area Formula.
  • SSA: Compare side \(a\) with altitude \(h = b \sin A\):
    • \(a < h \implies 0\) solutions
    • \(a = h\) or \(a \ge b \implies 1\) unique solution
    • \(h < a < b \implies 2\) distinct solutions
Final Takeaway: Master the Casio fx-50FH Prog 2 execution rules and always verify physical feasibility when working with ambiguous SSA cases!
